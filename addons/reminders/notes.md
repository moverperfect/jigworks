The app emails reminders at set times with `reminders` from `src/server/reminders.ts`: a Durable Object, `REMINDERS`, that keeps them and sets its alarm for the next one due, and sends each once with `sendEmail` from the email add-on. Use it for any email to someone at a time the app keeps, such as the day before each run they signed up for, rather than an alarm of your own. An email on a timetable, such as a rota every Monday, is an alarm of your own that sets the next, as the alarms notes say.

- **Keeping one:** `await reminders(c.env.REMINDERS).add({ to, about, subject, text, at })`, where `to` is the address, `about` names the thing it's about, such as the run's ID, and `at` is when it's due, an instant in milliseconds. It returns the reminder as kept, with `sent: false`. `remove(to, about)` removes one, such as when someone drops out or the thing is cancelled, and `list()` gives every reminder, with `sent`, and `error` saying why one never will be.
- **One reminder per person per thing:** adding one with the same `to` and `about` replaces it, rather than adding another, so signing up twice doesn't send two. One already sent isn't sent again unless its `at` changes, and one that failed is tried afresh. When a thing's time changes, add its reminders again with the new time. For two reminders about one thing, such as a week before and the day before, give each its own `about`, such as `run-12/week` and `run-12/day`.
- **Time zones:** the platform's clock is UTC, so never make a time with `new Date("2026-12-05T09:00")` or `setHours`. Keep the Developer's time zone in `wrangler.jsonc`'s `vars` as its name, such as `"TIME_ZONE": "Europe/London"`, and make each instant with `instantAt(date, time, c.env.TIME_ZONE)`, such as `instantAt("2026-12-05", "09:00", c.env.TIME_ZONE)`, which follows summer time. Keep a thing's date as the Developer or the page gave it, such as `"2026-12-06"`; the day before is `new Date(Date.UTC(2026, 11, 6 - 1)).toISOString().slice(0, 10)`. When you don't know the Developer's time zone, ask with `ask`, with answers in plain words, such as "UK time" for Europe/London, before you set any reminder.
- **When they're sent:** each is sent when it's due, usually up to a minute late. One whose time has passed when it's added goes about a minute later, so check `at > Date.now()` before adding one if it shouldn't go then. A ring sends up to 40, and the next ring, a minute later, the next 40. Each is marked sent as soon as Resend takes it, so a retry sends only those not sent. A ring cut off between the two, such as by new code, can send that one twice.
- **Failures:** each is logged with the reminder's `id`. One that couldn't be sent, such as when Resend couldn't be reached or was busy, or `RESEND_API_KEY` isn't set yet, is tried again with `alarm()`'s retries, 7 tries over about an hour, then kept with its `error`. While those retries wait, reminders that come due wait with them. One Resend refused, such as to an address other than the Developer's own while the app uses Resend's test sender, isn't tried again. Adding either again, unchanged, tries it afresh.
- **The Preview sends real email:** its reminders are sent by themselves, as the Deployment's are, with the same Secrets, to real people. Say so to the Developer, and suggest they use their own address, in place of the people the app reminds, while they try it. To try one, add a reminder due a minute or two ahead with `request_preview`, wait, then read `preview_logs` and the reminder's `sent`.
- **Tests** don't get the `REMINDERS` binding. `fakeReminders()`, from `src/server/fake-reminders.ts`, keeps reminders in memory and sends none: pass it in a route's test, as `app.request(path, init, { STORE: fakeStore(), REMINDERS: fakeReminders() } as Env)`, then read them with `reminders(env.REMINDERS).list()`.
